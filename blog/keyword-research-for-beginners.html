Keyword Research for Beginners

By Waru • September 8, 2026 • 10 min read

Introduction

Keyword research is the foundation of every successful SEO strategy. Before you can optimise a page, you need to know what words and phrases your target audience actually types into Google.

But keyword research doesn't happen in a vacuum. To understand why certain pages rank and others don't, you first need to understand two critical processes: crawling and indexing.

What is Crawling?

Crawling is how search engines discover content on the web. Google uses automated programs called crawlers (also called spiders or bots — most notably Googlebot) to browse the internet by following hyperlinks from page to page.

How Googlebot crawls a site

  1. Googlebot starts from a list of known URLs (from previous crawls, sitemaps, or submitted links).
  2. It visits each page, reads its content, and follows all the links it finds.
  3. Newly discovered links are added to the crawl queue.
  4. The cycle repeats continuously — Google recrawls popular pages more often.

What can block crawling?

  • A robots.txt file that disallows bots
  • Broken or missing internal links
  • Login walls or JavaScript-only content
  • Very slow page load times

What is Indexing?

After a page is crawled, Google decides whether to index it — that is, add it to Google's giant database of known pages. Only indexed pages can appear in search results.

What affects indexing?

  • Content quality: Thin or duplicate content may not be indexed.
  • Meta robots tag: <meta name="robots" content="noindex"> tells Google not to index the page.
  • Canonical tags: Tell Google which version of a URL is the "master" copy.
  • HTTP status codes: A 404 (Not Found) or 5xx error means the page won't be indexed.

What is robots.txt?

The robots.txt file sits at the root of your website (e.g., https://example.com/robots.txt) and instructs search engine bots which pages or directories they are allowed or not allowed to crawl.

Basic robots.txt syntax

# Allow everything (default)
User-agent: *
Allow: /

# Block a specific folder
User-agent: *
Disallow: /admin/

# Point to your XML sitemap
Sitemap: https://example.com/sitemap.xml

What is a Broken Link?

A broken link (also called a dead link) is a hyperlink that leads to a page that no longer exists or cannot be reached — typically returning a 404 Not Found error.

Why broken links hurt SEO

  • They waste crawl budget — Googlebot keeps trying to fetch pages that don't exist.
  • They block the flow of link equity (PageRank) through your site.
  • They create a bad user experience, increasing bounce rates.

How to fix broken links

  1. Use a tool like Screaming Frog or Google Search Console to detect broken links.
  2. If the content moved, set up a 301 redirect from the old URL to the new one.
  3. If the content is permanently gone, remove the link or replace it with a working alternative.

Keyword Research Step-by-Step

Step 1 – Brainstorm seed keywords

Start with a broad topic related to your niche. If you sell athletic gear, a seed keyword might be "running shoes".

Step 2 – Understand keyword metrics

  • Search Volume: How many times per month the keyword is searched. "running shoes" → ~450,000 searches/month.
  • Keyword Difficulty (KD): How hard it is to rank.
  • Search Intent: What does the user actually want? Informational, navigational, commercial, or transactional?

Step 3 – Find long-tail variants

Instead of targeting "running shoes" (very competitive), look for long-tail keywords:

  • "best running shoes for flat feet"
  • "lightweight trail running shoes under ₹5000"
  • "how to choose running shoes for beginners"

Step 4 – Analyse the competition

Search your target keyword and examine the top 10 results. Ask:

  • What type of content is ranking?
  • How long are the articles?
  • How many backlinks do the top pages have?
  • Can I create something significantly better?

Step 5 – Map keywords to pages

Each page on your site should target one primary keyword and a handful of semantically related terms.

Free Keyword Research Tools

  • Google Search Console – see which queries you already rank for
  • Google Keyword Planner – search volume and CPC data
  • Ubersuggest – free tier with keyword ideas
  • AnswerThePublic – question-based keyword discovery
  • Google Autocomplete – just start typing in the search bar!

Summary

Before you can rank for keywords like "running shoes", Google must first crawl and index your pages. Your robots.txt file guides bots, and broken links can silently kill your rankings. Once the technical foundation is solid, use keyword research to find realistic, high-intent terms you can actually compete for.
